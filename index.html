The Structure: Why a Trust, Not a Corporation

The trust is the oldest and most durable instrument of collective private governance in the English-speaking world. It predates the corporation by four centuries. Its essential character is unchanged from the 12th-century equity courts: legal title vests in trustees of proven integrity; beneficial interest belongs to members. No public filings. No activist shareholders. No quarterly earnings calls to manage.

  • Member assets remain under member control — the Trust coordinates, not commands
  • Governance by a Council of Trustees elected from and by member enterprises
  • Benefits flow to members in proportion to productive contribution, not financial leverage
  • Exit rights preserved — membership is voluntary, sovereign, and perpetually renewable
  • No ESG mandates, diversity quotas, or ideological conditions on capital access
